When two prediction markets disagree about Bitcoin, the disagreement is a stablecoin.

Polymarket settles on Binance BTC/USDT. Kalshi settles on a US-dollar index. The wedge between them is the USDT/USD basis — signed, and knowable before the hour resolves.

02 — THE WINDOW

Both feeds roam — and both legs win

Through the hour the two feeds move together, locked a stablecoin apart. Each dot must finish in its own colour: Binance above the open (Polymarket Up) and the index below the strike (Kalshi No). When the basis flips inside the hour the feeds cross — each dot lands in the other's zone, and both legs lose. Pick an outcome, or watch one hour play out.

04 — THE RESULT

Read the gap, and the loss disappears

Held every hour over 1,350 real settlements, here is how each rule's outcomes fall out. The naive rule ignores the offset and sends 4.6% of hours to a double loss. The offset-aware rule — strike above the open, direction from the USDT sign — empties that band and, as a by-product, widens the double-win one.

Research note

What this is, and isn't

A structural identification: the Polymarket–Kalshi settlement gap on hourly crypto is the USDT/USD stablecoin basis, and reading it removes a double-loss tail — validated on real Kalshi settlements. It is not a claim of profits; the deep riskless windows sit below what a retail poller can catch, but the positive-expectation ones are measured and replayed in the interactive above.
